## The conversation-mode answer (as delivered)

> Look at what the market just told you: the audit itself is being given away. Two of your competitors run free cost audits as bait for the implementation work behind them. So "what should I charge for the audit" is probably the wrong question — the real question is what the audit is *for*. If it's your product, you're entering a market that's pricing it at zero. If it's your hook, zero is the right price and you're already ahead, because yours is built.
>
> The people who do charge — $5k to $15k, fixed fee — get away with it by being productized and visibly decoupled from selling you anything afterward. Notice that's just accountability wearing a price tag: fixed scope, named deliverables, your reputation on the output.
>
> And the documented way people die in this market is under-scoping to win the deal — the data mess is most of the work and nobody prices it in. So whatever you promise in a free teardown, make it something you can deliver in the hours you actually have.
>
> Barbell it. One end: the free teardown, bounded cost, thirty sends, your name on every report — that's your reputation flywheel and your market research running at once. Other end: a fixed-price implementation sprint for whoever the teardown converts. What you don't do is the fragile middle — a $2k audit is too expensive to be bait and too cheap to live on.
>
> You already know the next move, and it isn't pricing. Would ten of the thirty say yes to the free teardown this week? The market answers pricing questions faster than I can. Send.
